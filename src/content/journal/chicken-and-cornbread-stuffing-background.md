This recipe has been a great way to prep for Thanksgiving, although it uses the less traditional option of chicken. These principles can still be applied to turkey with more olive oil, or perhaps by swapping butter to account for the leaner nature of the meat. An olive oil coating, thorough seasoning, and a minimal yet effective honey-butter glaze make for a perfectly sweet and savory blend, bringing the cumin, cinnamon, and paprika all together. This chicken throws the idea of minimal flavors out the window in favor of a complex but very satisfying experience.

The cornbread stuffing is, of course, a twist on a classic, but stands on its own as a whole different dish. This is a recreation of a recipe my father and I would eat growing up at my local, trashy buffet. With its shutdown, there’s no comparing mine to the original or even to the actual quality of the original. But my memory of the dish, at least, is a high bar to pass. There’s nothing very out of the ordinary here: I follow many traditional flavors you’d expect in a stuffing, but let the homemade cornbread speak for itself.

As for the cranberry sauce, some prefer homemade, and some prefer the canned classic. There’s no judgment either way, and I’m not critical of those who prefer canned. With this recipe, however, a fresher cranberry sauce better suits the savory and complex happenings of the rest of the dish.
